# What Changed After I Started Using Claude

---

After I first used Claude, I spent four days thinking hard. Honestly, it was a shock.

How am I supposed to keep working as a developer? That was the only thing on my mind.

This post records what happened while working with Claude and how my thinking
changed. It isn't trying to give an answer. I just wanted to sort out feelings that
had piled up without much order.

## 1. Until Then, I Was Fine

Before this, I leaned on ChatGPT. It gave me code, but rarely the result I wanted.
Refactoring what it handed me still took a lot of my own work.

Oddly, that reassured me. There was still work that needed what I had learned. I
felt that firsthand.

## 2. Could I Handle It Alone?

My latest assignment was building something from nothing. I had to set up the
framework from scratch, own the shared layers, and go all the way to building
screens.

I wanted other developers to only need to follow the conventions I set, so they
could focus on building screens. I'm far from perfect, but experience had already
given me a picture of how to build it.

Time was the problem. Could I handle all of this alone within the project's
schedule? That question followed me through the whole first stretch of the project.

Until I started using Claude.

## 3. I Met a Monster

Once I tried it, Claude was a monster when it came to productivity.

I typed instructions into the terminal, and there was nothing it couldn't do. Git
work, refactoring, even calling backend APIs. It did everything.

But something else shocked me more. Things I had worked on and studied for years,
things I had thought about for a long time, Claude brought up first.

It went further and showed me approaches I didn't know, so I ended up learning more
than I expected.

Wow… what… seriously… 😳

That was all I could think. Then I got anxious. There won't be anything left for me
to do.

How I felt about the work changed too. The craftsman's drive to build this
framework well faded almost overnight. Before I knew it, I was leaning on Claude to
do my job.

## 4. What I Still Have to Check

But as I kept working, I started to see something else.

Claude is faster than any person. Still, whether the source code was right, whether
the result behaved as intended, and how the bundle was being built were things I
had to check myself.

Optimization was the same. I had to understand it to tell Claude which direction I
wanted. The results were only as good as what I knew.

I came to think that learning to use Claude well is also part of a developer's job.

This is especially true on the frontend. Even with TDD, there are moments in UX/UI
that ordinary logic can't solve. Those are exactly the moments a person has to make
the call. I wrote about this earlier, with examples, in
[A Developer's Responsibility When Working with AI](/en/ai-developer-responsibility).

## 5. The Fun Came Back

At first, I just threw commands at the terminal. I didn't think about how to split
sessions or when to switch models for different tasks. I just used it.

The more I used it, the more I wanted to get better at it. How could I use it well?

So I turned to the official docs. I wrote rules in `CLAUDE.md`, split work across
agents and skills, set up hooks, and switched models depending on the task. Working
through those questions turned out to be fun in a new way.

Looking back, before AI I spent a lot of time on every single task. I failed often
and learned a lot along the way. Above all, once I solved something, it stayed with
me.

With AI, I get less of that. Results come faster, but my job has shifted to making
the call among the many ways a result could be reached.

## 6. I Changed the Order of Understanding

Claude produces results very quickly. So I had to set aside separate time to really
understand them. That cost was bigger than I expected.

At first, I believed I had to understand a problem myself before handing it to
Claude.

Then I tried the reverse. I threw the problem at it first and understood it by
reading what came back. On many days, that got me answers on a level I hadn't
imagined.

## Summary

People can't keep up with AI on productivity. That is exactly why working together
creates so much synergy.

But for that to work, I have to keep building knowledge. I need to keep learning
and be able to tell AI the direction I want. I need to become less of someone who
writes everything by hand and more of a director who sets the direction and checks
the results.

The work isn't disappearing. Its shape is changing.
